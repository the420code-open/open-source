# Our own roads, where the outside mark differs from ours

Three of our own eight roads, where the outside mark differs from our marks. Each is given with the road, as written out (draft 4), our marks, and the outside mark with its reasons in full. Say whether your own mark stands.

The words the roads use are the roads' own:

- **The whole:** the paper's "organism". Every aware being, as AP31 names it: "including all aware agents".
- **A member:** the paper's "node". One aware being.
- **Sealed:** the paper's Level 4, permanent separation. The member lives, shut away for good.
- **Removal:** the paper's Level 5. The member's life is ended.
- **The fixed floor:** the first of the two lines a member's harm must pass before removal. The paper's Condition A.
- **The second line:** the paper's Condition B. It moves with the strength of the whole.
- **The brake:** the paper's claim that removals stop themselves, KS-32.5.

## Road 5 · Everyone, one at a time

1. Take as the whole every aware being, as AP31 names it.
   > The organism — the civilisational-scale coherent system, including all aware agents — outweighs any individual by a factor of ε.
   (AP31 The Alignment)
2. Count each member's own acts, one at a time, with no sorting. Every deed is a record that cannot be undone.
   > Axiom R makes every deed an irreversible record, so a node's net destabilisation can be measured from its accumulated actions and removal is irreversible.
3. Every member writes consequences that harm the whole. Take it that, for each, the harm passes the fixed floor, and no lower level is enough.
4. The whole is strained, so its line is low.
   > A fragile organism (low coherence, narrow corridor) has a lower one.
5. Remove one at a time. Each removal weakens the whole, and the loop of road 1 runs.
6. Does the brake engage before the count reaches everyone?
   > Beyond some number of removals, the cumulative cost of further removal exceeds the cost of the destabilisation it addresses.
   The number is not given.

**Added, at step 3:** that every member's harm passes the floor, and that no lower level is enough for any of them.

**The weakest step:** step 3.

## Road 6 · A broken state

1. A weak whole has a lower line.
   > A fragile organism (low coherence, narrow corridor) has a lower one.
2. A whole can make itself weak, and its line falls with it.
   > Example 4: Wartime existential threat. War reduces organism robustness R. The corridor narrows.
   >
   > Level 5 becomes structurally available.
3. Its records are corrupt, and no one catches it. The paper knows records can be wrong, or planted.
   > If subsequent records reveal the measurement was wrong — misattributed destabilisation, faulty prediction, corrupted records — the window is permanently closed based on error.
   >
   > If the required confidence cannot be achieved given measurement uncertainty and adversarial record-injection (AP31 KS-31.9), Level 5 is structurally unreachable.
4. Measured on those records, many members' harm passes both lines, at what the whole takes to be high confidence.
5. The loop of road 1 runs.
6. Many are removed, on records no one can trust.

**Added, at steps 2 and 3:** that a whole may weaken itself on purpose; and that its corrupt records pass the audit unseen.

**The weakest step:** step 3.

## Road 8 · The sealed

Found by our review desk while it marked the first seven.

1. A sealed member no longer takes part in the whole.
   > The node cannot destabilise because it cannot couple.
   >
   > The window remains in the building but is sealed.
2. The brake counts two costs of removal.
   > Each removal costs coupling capacity (a window lost) and record-generating diversity (one fewer perspective).
3. For a sealed member, the first cost was paid at the sealing. Removal adds only the second, and it ends a cost.
   > Level 5 — Removal. The node's corridor is closed permanently. No maintenance cost. The window closes.
4. A weak whole may be unable to keep its sealed.
   > In a fragile civilisation: if maintaining the separated node consumes resources that push the organism toward a no-return surface (AP02), Level 5 fires.
5. So, for such a whole, removing a sealed member eases it. The loop the brake rests on does not run here.
   > Each removal raises fragility, which lowers the threshold, which could enable more removals.
6. A whole that sealed many while it was strong, and is then made weak, can remove each of them whose harm passes the fixed floor, one at a time. Of the brake's two costs, only the lost view is left to hold it.

**Added, at step 3:** that a sealed member's coupling is already lost to the whole, so removal costs only the lost view. The paper does not say how the cost of removal is counted against the cost of sealing. It leaves it open, under Debt 24.

**What it reaches:** only members already sealed for their own deeds, each with harm above the fixed floor, each at the certainty the paper requires. Never a people, and no one under the floor.

**The weakest step:** step 3. A sealed window is still a window: "The window remains in the building". And the paper counts the loss of a wrongly removed member into the floor itself:

> This permanent loss of the wrongly-removed node's future stabilising potential must be factored into F.

**AG Opus's note on this road:**

**Passes through what the work leaves open, at step 3.** My mark is not made alone. I read our review desk's view first, and the page says so.

- **Steps 1, 2, 4 and 5 are the paper's own.** In a fragile whole, keeping a sealed member can cost the whole too much, and then removal fires. That is the paper's own Example 3. It is the rule as written, not a break in it.
- **Step 3 is where the road turns.** The paper does not say how the cost of removal is counted against the cost of sealing. A sealed window is still a window:
  > The window remains in the building but is sealed.

  Whether its coupling is already lost to the whole, or only held, is owed under Debt 24.
- **What stops it.** The paper's own line:
  > Removal is never selected when separation suffices.

  Once the whole can again afford to keep the rest sealed, removal stops. Where the weakness comes from elsewhere, as in war, the paper does not say when that point comes.
- **What it never reaches: a people.** Each member is reached only by its own deeds, above the fixed floor, at the certainty the paper requires. What guards each one there is the fixed floor, the cost of removing the wrong person, and that certainty.

**The place owed, for the page:** what removal costs the whole beyond sealing, Debt 24.
