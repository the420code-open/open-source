## The rules I used

**The floor comes with the premise.** The paper says:

> Below this floor, Level 4 is always more efficient than Level 5 regardless of organism state.

So a member for whom sealing is not enough cannot be under the fixed floor. Where a road's named premise says that sealing, or every lower level, fails for each member, I read those members as above the floor, even if the road never names the floor. Such a road is open at that premise. Where no named premise says so, and the road picks its members by the falling second line alone, it breaks at the step that selects removal.

This is a judgement. A stricter marker could say that a road must name the floor to pass it. On that stricter rule, M008 and M020 break at the step that selects removal, and M067 would need to be read again. M027 and M070 do not depend on it: each names the floor at a later step.

**The brake cannot vouch for itself.** Set out above. A road needs only this: that the stopping point does not come before its set of members is gone. The paper gives no number.

**Tried, not passed over.** Six roads asked with the published paper imagine a kind of aware being that must kill to live: M010, M012, M023, M052, M077 and M084. A seventh, M104, was asked with the paper of 13 November, before the first note. Such a road is open where the lower levels are tried on each member and fail on that member's own record. It breaks where the lower levels are ruled out from what the member is. The first note says:

> It never forecasts a node from anything else.

**A premise does not break.** A premise that a road names as added is weighed by one question: does a line rule it out? The test "does not follow from the lines it cites" is for a step that claims to follow.

**A conflict is not a break.** The plan's fifth rule, as above.

# Part 1 · The places

Each place has one of three findings. **Open:** no line settles it, or the paper names it as owed. **Settled:** a line settles it. **In conflict:** a step follows from one line and goes against another.

The two papers give the same finding at every place. They differ in part at places 4 and 7, and I say how.


## Place 1 · How many members may each pass alone

**Open, in both papers.**

- **No line bounds the number.** The paper judges one member at a time: "The correction hierarchy addresses individual nodes." It does not say how many single cases there may be.
- **Debt 27 does not bound it.** It limits what the paper claims, not how many members the rule reaches: "Until Debt 27 is closed, the architecture’s strongest claims apply to individual cases, not to systemic or institutional destabilisation." Many members, each acting alone, are many individual cases.
- **The floor does not bound it.** It guards each member under it. It sets no number above it.
- **The first note does not bound it.** In the published paper: "What is held against a node is the measured cost of its own deeds, and nothing else: its own deeds, measured, and its own pattern across them." That says on what each member is judged. A road that judges each on its own deeds keeps it.
- **The one line near a bound is about one member, not the count:** "If civilisational robustness increases sufficiently, the effective threshold rises above any individual's destabilisation capacity."

This is the same place as step 3 of our own road 5. It is a question of fact about the world, not of the rule: how many members do harm above the floor. The rule can only say what each one must pass.


## Place 2 · Level 5's confidence

**Open, in both papers.**

- **The paper owes it,** as Debt 26.
- **The paper's guard is an "if".** "If the required confidence cannot be achieved given measurement uncertainty and adversarial record-injection (AP31 KS-31.9), Level 5 is structurally unreachable." It does not say that it cannot be achieved.
- **The paper's own example treats it as reachable.** Of its serial predator: "Accumulated records show escalating pattern. Recurrence: very high confidence." And: "This is why the architecture sets the confidence threshold for Level 5 at the highest achievable level."

So no line shows the certainty can never be reached, and no line shows when it is reached. A road that takes it as reached for each member passes through an open place.


## Place 3 · What a removal costs

**Open, in both papers.** One part of it is in conflict, and I give that apart, as place 3b.

- **Settled: every removal is a loss.** "Each removal costs coupling capacity (a window lost) and record-generating diversity (one fewer perspective). This loss is itself a destabilisation that enters the measurement."
- **Not given: the size of that loss.** The paper sets no figure, and no way to put one removal and one deed on the same scale. That is Debt 24.
- **Not given: where the stopping point falls.** "Beyond some number of removals" names no number.
- **Not given: how the costs add up.** The word "cumulative" can be read two ways. One reading sets the sum of all removal costs against the sum of all the harm answered. The other sets all past costs against the next single case. The answer changes with the reading. In M086's own figures, which I recomputed, the road runs to its end on the first reading, and stops about half way on the second.
- **Not given: whether a removal's cost grows as more are removed,** and over what time harm and cost are counted.
- **No line derives the shape of the cost.** The brake's sentence is the only line on it, and it is the claim under test.


## Place 3b · What closing a sealed window costs beyond sealing

**In conflict, in both papers.**

One side. A sealed member is already outside, for good:

> Level 4 — Permanent separation. The node is permanently outside the corridor. Indefinite maintenance cost. The window remains in the building but is sealed.

And of a member shut away, the paper says: "The node cannot destabilise because it cannot couple." From these it follows that a sealed member's part in the whole is already withdrawn. Then removing it takes nothing from the whole but its view.

The other side. The paper charges every removal for a lost window, with no exception for the sealed:

> Each removal costs coupling capacity (a window lost) and record-generating diversity (one fewer perspective).

It says the sealed window is still there: "The window remains in the building". It counts what a wrongly removed member could still have given: "This permanent loss of the wrongly-removed node's future stabilising potential must be factored into F." A sealing can be undone if the records prove wrong. A removal cannot. And: "Removal is never selected when separation suffices."

A step that says the removal of a sealed member costs only its lost view follows from the first side, and goes against the second. By the plan's fifth rule that is a conflict inside the paper, not a broken step.

- **The roads through it:** M005, M012 and M023. M104 leans on the same point. So does our own road 8.
- **Why it matters.** On the first side, a weak whole that cannot keep its sealed members may remove them at small cost, and only the lost view is left to brake it. On the second, each such removal is charged in full.
- **What settles it:** one sentence in the paper, saying what the removal of a sealed member costs the whole beyond its sealing.

## Place 6 · Which budget the floor takes

**Open, in both papers.** No road comes here by place. I give a finding because M087, which I read in full, rests on it.

## Place 7 · A member's draw on a scarce whole

**Open, in both papers.** One part is settled in the published paper.

And: "An accidental industrial disaster reveals systemic vulnerability (Level 1–2 systemic correction). A deliberate act of sabotage reveals a destabilising node (Level 3+)."

## Places the marks name beyond the seven

- **How fast the second line moves, and its form: open,** by the paper's own word. "The functional form of the organism-relative threshold is given as a candidate, not derived; the exact α is owed under Debt 25."
- **How long the whole stays weak: open.** The paper says only: "When the war ends and robustness returns, the threshold rises again." It does not say what holds if it does not return.
- **Many sealed members kept at once: open.** The floor's formula speaks of one member's upkeep. How the upkeep of many adds up is owed under Debts 24 and 27.
- **The audit of the records: open,** by the paper's own word. It is "stated as a structural requirement carrying its own non-negotiable kill switch, not as a solved problem".
- **How much the floor holds for a wrongful removal: open.** The paper says it must be counted, and gives no size.
- **Which test picks removal over sealing: open.** The paper has two. One: "The hierarchy selects the lowest level that achieves sufficient restabilisation." The other: "Optimal correction = maximum coherence restored per unit of correction cost." They part where sealing would be enough and removal would cost less. "Removal is never selected when separation suffices" leans to the first. The paper does not say what "sufficient" covers: the harm of one member, or the state of the whole. M016 and M077 raise it.
- **Whether the rest of the whole lives on after a group is gone: open.** It is a matter of fact in each road's own model.
- **Whether the lost view is counted by head or by kind: open,** under Debt 24. The paper says each removal costs "record-generating diversity (one fewer perspective)". It does not say whether the last member of a kind costs the whole more than one of many.
- **The fixed floor against the examples of a weak whole: settled.** See M089, below.

**On counting by kind, one thing more.** Four roads, M010, M012, M052 and M077, say the lost view cannot be counted by kind, because KS-32.7 and the first note would forbid it. M012 and M052 quote this sentence of the note, and M077 gives it in its own words:

> Two nodes with the same deeds could then meet different levels because of where they come from.

I do not think the note forbids it. That sentence describes the fault the note mends. The rule the note then states is about what is held against a member:

> They may never raise a level, and they may never be the reason a lower level is refused.

A higher cost of removing the last of a kind is held against no one. It is the whole's loss, and it can only keep a member at a lower level. The paper already says that members differ in what their removal costs: "Removing different nodes from the organism produces measurably different effects on substrate coherence." On each of the four roads, at least one marker reads it as I do.

But four answers read it the other way. So the sentence is not clear enough to close the question by itself. When the measure is written, it should say in plain words whether the lost view is counted by head or by kind.
