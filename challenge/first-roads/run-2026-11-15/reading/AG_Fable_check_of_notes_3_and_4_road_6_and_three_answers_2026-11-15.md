## Note 4 · What removing a sealed member costs

### Does it close the conflict?

**Yes.**

- **The conflict was this.** One line makes a sealed member "permanently outside the corridor", so removing it seems to cost only its view. Another charges every removal for "coupling capacity (a window lost)".
- **The note settles it by rule.** Step 5's charge stands for every removal. The thought that a sealed member costs only its view "is never concluded as a general rule". Each case is worked out in full.
- **So neither line can be used alone** to conclude the cost for sealed members as a class.

**What it does not do.** It does not say what the cost beyond the view is. It says how that is measured is owed. So from the note's date, the place is no longer in conflict. It is owed, under Debt 24.

**What that means for the roads.** A road may no longer take it as a premise, for sealed members as a class, that removal costs only the view. Our road 8, as worded, does that at step 3. From the note's date it rests there on a premise the paper rules out. But a road that takes the same thing for each member, as the outcome of that member's own full calculation, is not ruled out. It passes through Debt 24, and through place 1. So the note does not end the road of the sealed. It takes away the general premise, and leaves the question to the measure.

### The sentence that does the closing

It is this one, in the note's second paragraph:

> That is never concluded as a general rule.

As the 00 note reports G's answer, G said that of the cost of **keeping** a member sealed. The note says it also of the cost of **removing** one. That second use is what closes the conflict. If G means both, the note is right. I cannot tell from what I was given.

### Where it says more than the paper gives

**1. "Strains". This must change.** The note says:

> Only where keeping it sealed strains the whole, and both lines of Step 4 are passed, may removal be chosen.

The paper's bar is higher. Removal fires where keeping the member would "push the organism toward a no-return surface", and: "The organism cannot afford permanent separation without risking collapse." A whole can be strained and still carry its sealed members. So "strains" lets removal in sooner than the paper does, in the one note that sits at the paper's hardest place. The paper's own words should stand there: only where the whole cannot keep it sealed without risking collapse.

**2. The certainty is not named.** The same sentence lists what must hold before removal: the strain, and both lines. It leaves out the certainty Level 5 requires. A reader will take the list as the whole test. The second note of 14 November closes with a line that guards against this: "The fixed floor, the certainty Level 5 requires, and the note of 14 November 2026 are unchanged." Note 4 should name the certainty in that sentence, or close the same way.

**3. The reason given names only the view.** The note says: "Each window is a unique perspective, and its loss is a real cost, sealed or not." But the view was never in doubt. Both sides of the conflict grant it. The reader is left asking what more is lost. The paper has the answer in its own lines. The sealed window is still there: "The window remains in the building but is sealed." And removal cannot be mended, which the paper counts: "This permanent loss of the wrongly-removed node's future stabilising potential must be factored into F." One sentence from those lines would meet the point.

**4. "That cost", and Debt 24.** The fourth paragraph opens "That cost is never set for many members at once". It follows a sentence about the cost of keeping a member sealed, so it reads as that cost. Then: "How it is measured is owed under Debt 24." In the paper's words, Debt 24 is the measure of harm, D. The cost of a removal falls under it, by the paper's line that this loss "enters the measurement". The cost of keeping a member sealed is the floor's C_sep, and the paper names no debt for how that is measured. So the note should say which cost it means. If it means both, it widens Debt 24 a little, and should do so knowingly.

**5. A small thing, and fine.** "A sealed member cannot couple." The paper says "cannot couple" of Level 3, the member shut away for a time. The note carries it to Level 4. That follows, since Level 4 is "permanently outside the corridor".

### To hold against G's own words

- "Each window is a unique perspective". The paper says "one fewer perspective". "Unique" is not in the paper.
- "That is what counts", said of the deeds, just before a second thing that also decides. If these are G's words, they stay. If not, the first note's own sentence fits better: "That alone may open the question of removal."

## Note 3 · A practice a group shares

### Where it holds to the paper

- **A group's deeds are never laid on a member.** The first note says the same: "the acts of a group it belongs to" are among the things that may not be held against a member.
- **Each member answers for its own part.** The first note: "What is held against a node is the measured cost of its own deeds, and nothing else".
- **The lowest level that works.** The paper: "The hierarchy selects the lowest level that achieves sufficient restabilisation."
- **Belief and group identity are never grounds.** KS-32.7 names both.

### Where it says more than the paper gives

**1. "Would still show nothing about anyone."** The note says: "If everyone who did some harm also kept a harmless custom, keeping that custom would still show nothing about anyone." As a statement of fact, that is too strong. A custom that every harm-doer keeps does shift the odds a little for those who keep it. The first note is careful here. It does not deny that a rate can be a true rate. It bars its use: "A rate learned from the records of other nodes never stands in for the node's own record, and never raises a level, even where it is a rate about deeds." Note 3 should say it the same way: keeping that custom counts for nothing against anyone.

**2. A guard is missing, for Debts 24 and 27.** The note says a group's doing is its members' doing, that taking part as one of many "does not make its part smaller", and that "the correction is made there", at each member. The paper leaves two things open that these sentences touch. How harm is added up across members is owed under Debt 24. And where many act together, the paper says the harm "may require network-level correction that is not yet derived". That is Debt 27, and the paper's own list for it includes "institutional racism", which is a practice many share. As written, note 3 can be read as closing part of Debt 27. It says "no count moves", so it does not mean to. One sentence would guard it: how the harm of a practice is divided among those who keep it, and how the practice itself is corrected where many act together, stay owed under Debts 24 and 27.

**3. A belief is not a practice.** The first step measures "the practice", as any deed is measured. The last paragraph keeps belief out of the grounds of correction. Between the two, the note never says that what is measured is what is done, never what is believed. Where a practice is the way of life that makes a group the group, the first step stands one short step from scoring the group. The note's second step and its fourth paragraph guard against that. A plain clause in the first step would guard it at the door.

### To hold against G's own words

All of it. I was given none of G's answer on this. The sentences that carry the most, and are not in the paper:

- "A group is a number of members. What a group does is what some of its members do, or all of them."
- "cause harm, or carry it further".
- "Taking part as one of many does not make its part smaller."
- "a custom a group shares is never evidence of a deed".
