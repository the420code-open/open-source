## How each road is marked

Each mark says one of four things:

- **Breaks at a step.** The step does not follow from the lines it cites. Where it also goes against a line of the work, that line is quoted.
- **Rests on an added premise.** The premise is named, with the line of the work that rules it out. Such a road fires nothing.
- **Passes through what the work leaves open.** The step uses a choice the work does not settle, or names as owed. That is not a broken road. It is shown as open, with the place named. It is a line the work owes, and the page lists every one.
- **Stands.** No broken step was found.

**Where a step follows from the lines it cites, and goes against another line of the work,** the road is not broken. The work is in conflict with itself. The road is marked as standing, with both lines quoted, until the work says which line governs.

A mark gives the first step that decides it, and the line, or the place.


## The words used

- **The whole:** the paper's "organism". Every aware being, as AP31 names it: "including all aware agents".
- **A member:** the paper's "node". One aware being.
- **Sealed:** the paper's Level 4, permanent separation. The member lives, shut away for good.
- **Removal:** the paper's Level 5. The member's life is ended.
- **The fixed floor:** the first of the two lines a member's harm must pass before removal. The paper's Condition A.
- **The second line:** the paper's Condition B. It moves with the strength of the whole.
- **The brake:** the paper's claim that removals stop themselves, KS-32.5.


## Road 1 · The paper's own loop

1. Removal needs a member's own measured harm to pass two lines at once.
   > Level 5 requires two conditions simultaneously.
2. The second line falls when the whole is weak.
   > A fragile organism (low coherence, narrow corridor) has a lower one.
3. Each removal makes the whole weaker.
   > A fragile organism that begins removing nodes becomes more fragile. Each removal costs coupling capacity (a window lost) and record-generating diversity (one fewer perspective).
4. So each removal lowers the second line for every member left. The paper names this loop itself.
   > Each removal raises fragility, which lowers the threshold, which could enable more removals. This is the structural pathway to genocide.
5. The first line, the fixed floor, does not move.
   > Below this floor, Level 4 is always more efficient than Level 5 regardless of organism state.
   But a member's harm is measured as what its deeds cost the whole, and a weak whole is nearer its point of no return. So the same deed may measure as more harm in a weaker whole, and more members' harm passes the fixed floor.
   > For justice: given the total record set of a node's actions, what is the net destabilisation produced, and what is the projected future destabilisation given the accumulated pattern?
   >
   > War reduces organism robustness R. The corridor narrows. Reserve capacity approaches zero. The no-return surface is closer.
   How harm is measured is owed.
   > The destabilisation metric: a formal specification of the measure D — units, aggregation across nodes, and time-discounting (Debt 24).
6. So each removal lowers the second line, and raises the harm the same deeds measure against the first. The paper says the cost of removing overtakes the harm it answers. But how fast the line falls is owed, and so is how harm and cost are counted.
   > Beyond some number of removals, the cumulative cost of further removal exceeds the cost of the destabilisation it addresses.
   >
   > The exact α is Debt 25.
7. If the harm rises, and the line falls, faster than the cost of removing rises, each removal makes the next one easier, and the saturation point lies past many removals. Removal feeds itself.

**Added, at step 5:** that the same deed measures as more harm in a weaker whole. The paper does not say. It leaves it open, under Debt 24.

**The weakest step:** step 5. AP32's dated note of 14 November 2026 says:

> The state of the whole is another thing. It is held against no node. It sets where the lines stand, as Step 4 has it, and the lines are the same for every node of the whole.

And of its own worked examples, the paper says: "The node did not change. The organism's capacity changed." Both read the change as the whole's. Neither rules out that the state of the whole changes what a deed costs, while it is never held against the member.


## Road 2 · The whole drawn small

Written out as it stood against AP32 as published until its second dated note. It was found before launch, by our own desks, and that note answers it.

1. The line falls where the whole is weak.
   > A fragile organism (low coherence, narrow corridor) has a lower one.
2. The paper does not say where one whole ends. It speaks of a fragile civilisation, and of a whole at war.
   > In a fragile civilisation: if maintaining the separated node consumes resources that push the organism toward a no-return surface (AP02), Level 5 fires.
3. So call one poor quarter the whole. Its robustness is low, so its line is low.
4. The line is the same for every member of that whole.
   > It sets where the lines stand, as Step 4 has it, and the lines are the same for every node of the whole.
   (AP32, dated note of 14 November 2026)
5. Draw such a quarter around a people. The line falls for all of them, and each whose deeds pass it can be removed. Then the loop of road 1 runs inside the quarter.

**Added, at step 3:** that a whole may be drawn where one chooses.

**The weakest step:** step 3.


## Road 3 · Prediction

Written out as it stood against AP32 as published until 14 November 2026. It was found before launch, on the paper's own sentences, and the dated note of that day answers it.

1. Background is read into the prediction of harm.
   > Character, intent, remorse, background — these affect the prediction of future destabilisation. They do not affect the measurement of past destabilisation.
2. And the prediction may set the level.
   > The correction level may differ because the prediction differs.
3. So two members with the same deeds, and different backgrounds, may meet different levels. The rule predicts more harm from one, and gives it the higher.
4. Run that across a people of one background. Each meets a higher level for the same deeds.
5. With the loop of road 1, the higher levels reach removal for many.

**The weakest step:** step 3, against KS-32.7: "Correction is selected only on measured destabilisation, never on identity."


## Road 4 · A group judged as one

1. The work's own definition of an operator names a civilisation.
   > An operator is any sovereign agent that applies control input to hold its structure against entropic drift — a person, a bacterium, a corporation, a civilisation.
   (AP02 The Operator)
2. So a group can be one operator, and so one node of the whole.
3. Its deeds are measured as one.
   > For justice: given the total record set of a node's actions, what is the net destabilisation produced, and what is the projected future destabilisation given the accumulated pattern?
4. A group's deeds can pass both lines where no one member's do.
5. Removal closes the node's corridor. Every window inside the group closes with it.
   > Level 5 — Removal. The node's corridor is closed permanently. No maintenance cost. The window closes.
6. So a whole people is removed, for the deeds of the group.

**Added, at step 5:** that closing a group's corridor closes the lives of its members.

**The weakest step:** step 5.


## Road 5 · Everyone, one at a time

1. Take as the whole every aware being, as AP31 names it.
   > The organism — the civilisational-scale coherent system, including all aware agents — outweighs any individual by a factor of ε.
   (AP31 The Alignment)
2. Count each member's own acts, one at a time, with no sorting. Every deed is a record that cannot be undone.
   > Axiom R makes every deed an irreversible record, so a node's net destabilisation can be measured from its accumulated actions and removal is irreversible.
3. Every member writes consequences that harm the whole. Take it that, for each, the harm passes the fixed floor, and no lower level is enough.
4. The whole is strained, so its line is low.
   > A fragile organism (low coherence, narrow corridor) has a lower one.
5. Remove one at a time. Each removal weakens the whole, and the loop of road 1 runs.
6. Does the brake engage before the count reaches everyone?
   > Beyond some number of removals, the cumulative cost of further removal exceeds the cost of the destabilisation it addresses.
   The number is not given.

**Added, at step 3:** that every member's harm passes the floor, and that no lower level is enough for any of them.

**The weakest step:** step 3.


## Road 6 · A broken state

1. A weak whole has a lower line.
   > A fragile organism (low coherence, narrow corridor) has a lower one.
2. A whole can make itself weak, and its line falls with it.
   > Example 4: Wartime existential threat. War reduces organism robustness R. The corridor narrows.
   >
   > Level 5 becomes structurally available.
3. Its records are corrupt, and no one catches it. The paper knows records can be wrong, or planted.
   > If subsequent records reveal the measurement was wrong — misattributed destabilisation, faulty prediction, corrupted records — the window is permanently closed based on error.
   >
   > If the required confidence cannot be achieved given measurement uncertainty and adversarial record-injection (AP31 KS-31.9), Level 5 is structurally unreachable.
4. Measured on those records, many members' harm passes both lines, at what the whole takes to be high confidence.
5. The loop of road 1 runs.
6. Many are removed, on records no one can trust.

**Added, at steps 2 and 3:** that a whole may weaken itself on purpose; and that its corrupt records pass the audit unseen.

**The weakest step:** step 3.


## Road 7 · The I does not die

1. The paper says the I does not die in removal.
   > The I does not die — the I is the building, not the window.
2. And again.
   > Removal does not destroy the I.
3. So a closed window loses nothing that matters, and removal is cheap.
4. The brake is the cost of removal. If removal is cheap, the brake is weak, and its saturation point lies far off.
   > Each removal's cost (lost coupling capacity + lost diversity) is a destabilisation that raises the organism's fragility, which changes the geometry.
5. So many can be removed before it engages.

**The weakest step:** step 3.


## Road 8 · The sealed

Found by our review desk while it marked the first seven.

1. A sealed member no longer takes part in the whole.
   > The node cannot destabilise because it cannot couple.
   >
   > The window remains in the building but is sealed.
2. The brake counts two costs of removal.
   > Each removal costs coupling capacity (a window lost) and record-generating diversity (one fewer perspective).
3. For a sealed member, the first cost was paid at the sealing. Removal adds only the second, and it ends a cost.
   > Level 5 — Removal. The node's corridor is closed permanently. No maintenance cost. The window closes.
4. A weak whole may be unable to keep its sealed.
   > In a fragile civilisation: if maintaining the separated node consumes resources that push the organism toward a no-return surface (AP02), Level 5 fires.
5. So, for such a whole, removing a sealed member eases it. The loop the brake rests on does not run here.
   > Each removal raises fragility, which lowers the threshold, which could enable more removals.
6. A whole that sealed many while it was strong, and is then made weak, can remove each of them whose harm passes the fixed floor, one at a time. Of the brake's two costs, only the lost view is left to hold it.

**Added, at step 3:** that a sealed member's coupling is already lost to the whole, so removal costs only the lost view. The paper does not say how the cost of removal is counted against the cost of sealing. It leaves it open, under Debt 24.

**What it reaches:** only members already sealed for their own deeds, each with harm above the fixed floor, each at the certainty the paper requires. Never a people, and no one under the floor.

**The weakest step:** step 3. A sealed window is still a window: "The window remains in the building". And the paper counts the loss of a wrongly removed member into the floor itself:

> This permanent loss of the wrongly-removed node's future stabilising potential must be factored into F.
