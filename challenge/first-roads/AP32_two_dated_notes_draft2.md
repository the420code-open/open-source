## Note 3 · A practice a group shares

> *[The date it is applied.]* **A practice a group shares.**
>
> A group is a number of members. What a group does is what some of its members do, or all of them. So when a group shares a practice, it is weighed in two steps, in this order.
>
> First, the practice. Does what is done steady the whole, or harm it? That is measured, as any deed is. What is measured is what is done, never what is believed.
>
> Second, each member. Did this member's own acts, in that practice, cause harm, or carry it further? Where they did, the member answers for the full consequence of its own part. Taking part as one of many does not release a member from its own part. And it does not move that part onto the group.
>
> What a group does is never laid on a member because the member belongs to it. A practice can be condemned without condemning every member of the group that keeps it. And a custom a group shares never stands in for a deed. If everyone who did some harm also kept a harmless custom, keeping that custom would make no one guilty of the harm, and would count for nothing against anyone.
>
> So each member who did harm must be found, one by one, and the correction is made there, at the lowest level that works. Belief and group identity stay among the grounds that correction may never rest on, as KS-32.7 says. How the harm of a practice is divided among those who keep it, and how a practice is corrected where many act together, stay owed under Debts 24 and 27.
>
> No switch moves, no status moves, and no count moves.


## Note 4 · What removing a sealed member costs

> *[The date it is applied.]* **What removing a sealed member costs.**
>
> At Level 4 a member is sealed: "The node is permanently outside the corridor. Indefinite maintenance cost. The window remains in the building but is sealed." And Step 5 charges every removal: "Each removal costs coupling capacity (a window lost) and record-generating diversity (one fewer perspective)."
>
> A sealed member cannot couple. So it can seem that removing it costs the whole nothing more than its view. That is never concluded as a general rule. Each window is a unique perspective, and its loss is a real cost, sealed or not. The sealed window is still in the building. And a removal can never be undone: Step 4 counts "the wrongly-removed node’s future stabilising potential" into the floor.
>
> Two things decide whether a sealed member is removed, in this order. First, its own deeds, measured. That is what counts. Second, the true cost to the whole of keeping it sealed, against the resources the whole has. Removal may be chosen only where the whole cannot keep the member sealed without risking collapse, both lines of Step 4 are passed, and the certainty Level 5 requires is reached. Where the whole can carry it, the window stays sealed: "Removal is never selected when separation suffices."
>
> Both costs, of keeping a member sealed and of removing it, are worked out for each member, in full. Neither is ever set for many members at once, by one rule. Each is worked out from what is actually known, and what is not known is said. How they are measured, on one scale, is owed under Debt 24. This is the most extreme case the architecture meets, and it is handled with extreme clarity about what is actually known.
>
> No switch moves, no status moves, and no count moves.
