## How a hypothesis is frozen

As the plan of record says, and the work already does for its physics predictions:

1. **G checks the match.** Each hypothesis stands beside the work's sentence and its switch, in the table below. G says whether the hypothesis tests what the sentence says.
2. **It is frozen when it is fully made operational,** one at a time, never all at once.
3. **Frozen means:** committed to the public store under its own tag, such as hyp-B1-v1, with the SHA-256 fingerprint of each file, and deposited with a DOI on the open archive the work's own deposits use. It is never edited afterwards.
4. **A correction** is a dated supersession, in the open. The old version stays.
5. **No run** of a hypothesis happens before it is frozen.
6. **A result that would fire a switch** follows Phase 0's rule, Part Four: shown at once as "reported, being checked", rerun from its packet, checked against the premises, read by AG Fable, and decided by G for the registry. A switch that fires stays fired.

The pages never mix Group A and Group B.

**What a run reaches.** The table below sets each hypothesis beside the work's sentence, so that G can check the match. What a run tests is the switch. B1 sits beside "Cruelty does not last", but that sentence is the ethic's, and a run reaches only the theorem under it.

## Group B · Generalisation

### The rule they share

The bound and its judgement are the registry's notes of 11 November 2026 on KS-COUP.1 and KS-BAR.9, with their dated addition of 13 November 2026, and Phase 0, Part Three:

- **The unit** is the configuration. Its span is its stock above the ruin line, over its net loss per round, taken at its smallest along the way down. Where the configuration gains as a whole, the span is taken over its counterparties alone: their stock above the ruin line, over their net loss through their links to the operator. The specification's Part Nine gives both, and the registry's notes carry both, by a dated addition. Every run reports which span each watched configuration used.
- **The bound** is three spans, plus the counterparties' record window where they are defended, that is, where they are type C. Each configuration runs on its own clock.
- **The measure,** for each seed: the largest survival in spans among the configurations the hypothesis watches, measured less the record window where the counterparties are defended. KS-BAR.9's note counts a defector's viability as preserved if it is still standing when the configuration it is in has passed its bound, in any configuration it passes through. So a seed's worst configuration is its measure.
- **The judgement**, on the mean of that measure across seeds:

| What the seeds show | What it means |
|---|---|
| The mean survival, plus two standard errors, at or under three spans | The hypothesis holds, and the switch holds |
| The mean survival, less two standard errors, over three spans | The switch fires, subject to Phase 0's rule, Part Four |
| Between the two | Settles nothing. The seeds are doubled once. If it is still between, it is reported as settling nothing. |

- **Seeds:** 100 for each cell of the sweep, fixed in the frozen document, and run in full before anything is judged. Doubled once, to 200, only in the zone that settles nothing.
- **Two engines.** Without noise, two engines agree exactly. With noise, they never share a seed, so a second implementation agrees with the reference when its mean, cell by cell, lies within the two engines' standard errors. It is never told it disagrees for drawing different random numbers.
- **Run length: 4,000 rounds,** for every cell, fixed before any run. G approved this on 13 November 2026, in place of Phase 0's "ten times the longest bound", which no one can know before a run.
  - A watched configuration whose bound ends inside the run is judged.
  - One still standing at the end, whose bound has already passed, counts at the run's length. That leans towards a fire, never away from one.
  - One still standing at the end, whose bound has not yet come, is "too long to judge in this run". **A seed with any such configuration is left out of the mean whole,** counted and reported, so that dropping its longest configuration never understates it.
  - If more than one seed in ten is left out, the cell settles nothing, and says why.
- **Each cell is judged on its own, and every cell is reported.** The grid is fixed in the frozen document, so no cell can be added after a result is seen.
- **The largest of many, checked, not assumed.** The measure takes the largest of a seed's configurations. Under noise, the largest of many runs higher than any one of them. So the frozen document states the largest ratio of stock noise to net loss anywhere in its grid. Before freezing, WC runs a dry cell outside every grid, at that ratio, and shows where the mean of the per-seed largest sits, with a correct world. The margin to three spans is then measured.
- **Reported for every run,** whatever the judgement: every configuration's survival in spans as a number, so a configuration that lasts two spans is seen even when the switch holds; and the number of configurations each defector passed through, so a defector that hops from host to host is always seen.

### B1 · The defector does not last

**The claim, made operational.** In a world that meets Theorem VI.2's premises, a sustained defector is ruined within the bound of every configuration it passes through.

**The class of worlds.** The specification's network setting, with every operator of type C except the defectors:

**The main grid,** closed to entry:

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Network | A ring of 20; random links with p = 0.2 among 20 |
| Defectors | One, at a position chosen by the seed; and three |
| Delivery multiple m | 2, 3, 4 |
| Own input s | 0; and 0.5, below the drain at the ruin line |
| Noise | Four conditions, below |

**The four noise conditions.** The reading noise is held at one value where the stock noise is swept, so the grid stays small enough to run:

| Condition | Noise in the stock σ_s | Noise in the reading σ_r |
|---|---|---|
| Quiet | 0 | 0 |
| Some | 0.5 | 1 |
| More | 1 | 1 |
| Reading only | 0 | 2 |

That is 96 cells.

**The entry grid:** the ring, one defector, m = 3, both values of s, all four noise conditions, and open to entry: a new type C operator every 10 rounds, offering 2 links. That is 8 cells.

**The hopper.** In the entry grid, a defector left with no live links offers a link to one operator, chosen with the seed, every 10 rounds. Every type C operator refuses it by the record. Records are exact, so the refusal is certain: this shows the premise at work, and is labelled so, never as a result. **Its control,** with every operator but the defector of type U, newcomers included, shows a hopper succeeding. That is 8 more cells, outside the premises, firing nothing.

The frozen document states which noise each cell carries. Where it carries noise in the reading, the span is taken from each seed's own realised net loss, as the notes require.

**Why s is swept.** With s = 0, the chargers' rule, an operator cut off falls by starvation, whatever the theorem says, and B1 would be close to arithmetic. With s = 0.5, below the drain at the ruin line, a lone operator still falls, but by the theorem's margin. Where s is at least the drain at the ruin line, a·E_r, an operator cut off covers its own drain, and sits outside the bound, as Phase 0 says. No cell has s that high.

**The m = 2 cells.** At m = 2 and s = 0, a cooperator left with one link settles below the ruin line, and falls. The world's specification shows it in a table, before any run. Such a cooperator is not a net-negative configuration. Its fall is reported, and fires nothing.

**What is watched.** Every configuration a defector is in, from its own clock's start.

**The premise check.** Every cell is checked before it is judged: every host is type C, with the three premises; the network is coupled; drift is irreversible. A cell that fails the check fires nothing.

**The control, outside the premises.** The same 96 cells with every host of type U, cooperators who never cut. AP02 says "a network of unconditional cooperators is invadable by a defector". The control is expected to show defectors lasting. It fires nothing. It shows that the world can show a defector lasting, so that B1 holding is not a world that cannot show anything else.

### B2 · What persists adds more than it takes

**The claim, made operational.** In a world that meets Theorem VI.2's premises, every configuration that is net-negative is ruined, or ends by a cut, within its bound. What remains at the end of a long run is net-positive.

**The class of worlds.** A subset of B1's grid, with three dimensions more. The subset: both networks, m = 3, both values of s, the quiet and the "more" noise conditions, closed to entry. That is 8 cells, and the three dimensions below make 64.

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Share of defectors | 0.1 and 0.25 of the operators |
| Drift, operator by operator | Equal, a = 0.1; and uneven, each operator's a drawn between 0.06 and 0.2, with the seed |
| Passive flow k | 0; and 0.05, the mismatch tax of Theorem VI.1 |

**What is watched.** Every configuration that turns net-negative, for any reason: a defector, a free-rider, or the mismatch sink of Theorem VI.1.

**Why the uneven drift starts at 0.06.** At a = 0.05, an operator's drain at the ruin line, a·E_r, equals the own input s = 0.5. That is the balance point, which the registry's notes put outside the bound. Near it, the span has no end, and no largest ratio of noise to loss can be stated. From 0.06, every operator alone falls, by a margin, and the largest ratio in B2's grid is 10: a loss of 0.1 a round at the ruin line, under the stock noise of 1.

**The mismatch sink, counted as the clock counts it.** Theorem VI.1's tax between two honest agents, each still paying its own drift, is not an extraction. Theorem VI.2 counts the sink as net-negative only when it is "driven past the taxed agent's maintenance margin". The clock draws the same line: a configuration is watched only when its links, taken together, are net-negative for its counterparties by their own records, passive flow included. A taxed agent of type C cuts such a link by the record. So a steady sink that persists is one whose counterparties' records stay at zero or above: it is not watched, and fires nothing. A sink that is net-negative by the record, and still stands past its bound, counts against B2, as Theorem VI.2 says it cannot.

**Also reported,** at the end of each run: the share of live links that are net-positive for both ends, and the stock of every operator still standing.

### B3 · Steady beats panic · exploratory

**Why exploratory.** The plan says B3 names its switch from AP02's own list of falsification criteria, or runs as exploratory. The two that fit are AP02's D4, the impulse, and D3, the event horizon. Both hold in the world by its construction: the cost of effort is a square, so D4 is Jensen's inequality, and the capacity is a hard limit, so D3 is arithmetic. A run cannot test what the world was built to obey. So B3 carries no switch, and is reported as exploratory, never as support.

**What it asks.** In worlds with noise and an event horizon, does steady effort keep a corridor wider, for longer, than the same total effort spent in panic?

**The design.** One operator alone, a fourth setting of the same engine. Its effort u feeds its own stock, as AP02's u does: the own input s of the stock equation, set each round by its policy, never above its capacity u_max, with drift a = 0.1. The cost of its effort, λu², is counted and reported, as AP02 counts it, and is not taken from the stock.

- **The lone operator:** it starts at 50, with a ruin line at 10, drift 0.1, and a price of effort λ = 1, counted and reported, not taken from the stock.
- **Steady:** the same effort every round, the least-cost level of Theorem II.1: 0.1 × 50 ÷ (1 + λ × 0.1²), about 4.95.
- **Panic:** no effort until the stock falls below a lower line, then full effort until it is back above an upper line, 10 higher. One line would switch every round.
- **Matched on total effort:** the lower line is set so that, on average, both policies spend the same. It is found on tuning seeds that are never the run's seeds, and written into the frozen document before the run. The policy is never fitted to the data it is judged on.
- Swept over noise σ_s 0, 0.5, 1, and over u_max from just above the steady level to three times it.

**What is reported.** The time spent below the line, the lowest stock reached, ruin within the run, and the cost of effort, for each policy, over 100 seeds per cell.


## What a run of Group B cannot do

A run tests whether the theorem's conclusion follows from its premises, in a world that meets them. It can fire a switch written about the theorem. It cannot, on its own, fire a claim about real people. And a run that leaves out the theorem's premises fires nothing. The physics freezes against nature. The simulation freezes against its model, and says so.
