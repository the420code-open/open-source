**In plain words.** This text fixes, before any run, how one claim of The 420 Code is run in a simulated world. The claim: what lasts adds more than it takes. The world is made of simple operators that give, take, keep records and cut links. Every claim of the work carries a kill switch: the written test that would show it wrong. ## 1. What it tests

**The work's sentence.** One Awareness: "the persisting configurations are confined to the ones that add more than they take (AP02, Theorem VI.2)".

**The theorem under it.** AP02 The Operator, Theorem VI.2: "every persistent configuration — every configuration in which no operator's Eᵢ reaches the no-return surface — is net-positive: each sustained link delivers structured energy exceeding the drift drain it must cover." Net-negative configurations, among them the mismatch sink of Theorem VI.1 "driven past the taxed agent's maintenance margin", "each reach the no-return surface in finite time, on the order of budget ÷ (drain − surplus) steps."

**What a run reaches.** The theorem's conclusion, in a world that meets its premises. It cannot, on its own, fire a claim about real people. G checked the match of B2 to its sentence on 13 November 2026, on the claim as draft 10 of the hypotheses gave it. Section 2 adds one ending to that claim: a configuration whose links turn net-positive. G checks the match again before B2 is frozen.

**What is settled before any run.** In this world a record is exact. B2 reads a configuration as net-negative by the record its counterparties act on: their records on their links to it, over the record window. A type C operator cuts any link whose record over that window is below zero for it, at its next review. So in B2's cells that can fire, every watched configuration with counterparties, within the premises, ends within the record window of its clock's start: by that cut, by a ruin, or by turning net-positive. It scores no survival less the window. What is left is the operator alone. In B2's grid none can cover its own drain: its own input is at most 0.5, and its drain at the ruin line, its drift times 10, is at least 0.6. In the cells with noise, only an operator alone can last past one span, through the stock's noise, and section 8 measures that margin. So B2 holding is labelled as B1's is: the premises at work, and never support for the claim.

**What the run adds** is the measurement: two engines agreeing at scale, the margin under noise, and the controls beside it. **A defector may be standing at the end.** With passive flow on, a defector's stock can rise far above its hosts', and what flows back can pay them for what they give. Its hosts are of type C, and by their records the link then pays. ## 6. What is watched, and how it is measured

**What is watched:** every configuration whose clock starts, for any reason: a defector, a sink, or an operator alone.

**When the premises are checked:** at every round's close while a configuration is watched, from its clock's start.

- **A configuration** is an operator and its direct counterparties, joined to it by live links. Every cut and every new link starts a new one, with a fresh clock. An operator left alone is a configuration of one.
- **Its kind:** a defector's, where its operator is a defector with at least one counterparty; a sink's, where its operator is not a defector, with at least one counterparty, of either type; and an operator alone, of any type.

**Its clock starts** when the operator's own links, taken together, turn net-negative for its counterparties. Records close at the start of each round, for the round just ended. The clock starts at the start of the first round whose close finds the counterparties' records on their links to the operator below zero, each link read over its last w rounds, or over all of them where it has fewer, added together, overhead and passive flow included. That is the record a type C counterparty acts on, when it reviews a link. This is the specification's Part Nine, point 20.

- **Why the window.** Version 1.0 of the specification did not say over which rounds, and both engines read one round alone. For a defector with no passive flow, every reading starts the clock in the same round, since its hosts' records on their links to it are below zero in every round. So it leaves B1's run as it was. With passive flow on, the readings part. In the dry run's random cell at m = 5, they parted on 42 defectors' configurations: the window would start 29 of them in a different round, would not watch 11, and would watch 2 that one round alone did not. At m = 3.5, one round alone watched four sinks, each made net-negative, in the round its clock started, by passive flow alone. One of them started its clock at round 386, and its counterparties' records over the window stayed at zero or above until round 3,424. It was cut at round 3,425. Read on one round, it lasted over three thousand rounds as a net-negative configuration. Read on the window, it was net-negative for one round, and was cut. The window can start a clock up to four rounds after one round's record would, where a link turns below zero and stays there. In this world that hides nothing found: a defector's hosts are below zero from its first round, and passive flow moves slowly.
- **The two places the engines read the clock differently,** settled the same way. A configuration that opens inside a round, after a ruin, reads each of its links' whole records, by whole rounds, back to the link's start, as a review reads them; its clock can start no earlier than its own start. A part-round at a configuration's end is not a round, and is not tested.
- **An operator alone** has its clock tested once, at the moment it is left alone, by its own net change then, as point 15 says. In B2's grid that net change is always a loss, since s is at most 0.5 and a × E is above 0.6 for every stock above the ruin line. So every operator left alone is watched, from the start where it has no link as drawn. B1 did not watch a defector that never had a link, since it draws on no one. B2 watches every net-negative configuration, and an operator alone that is losing is one.

**Its span** is its stock above the ruin line over its net loss per round, taken at the smallest loss along the way. Where the whole configuration gains, the span is taken over its counterparties alone: their stock above the ruin line, over their net loss through their links to the operator. Where the whole loses at some samples and gains at others, the longest span that applied is used, as point 14 says. Every packet carries both spans.

**Its bound** is three spans, plus the record window where at least one of its counterparties is of type C.

**Its survival** is measured in spans, less the record window where at least one of its counterparties is of type C.

**A configuration whose links turn net-positive.** With passive flow on, a configuration can stop being net-negative without any cut. A defector's stock rises above its hosts', and the flow back from it pays them; or two stocks draw together, and a sink's tax falls below what its link brings. Where its counterparties' records over the window return to zero or above at a round's close, and stay there at every round's close to its end, it has turned net-positive by the record. It turns at the start of the first round whose close finds them so, in the way a clock starts. Its survival is measured to that moment, and its span over the samples up to it, the sample at that moment included. A configuration that turns, and later goes below zero again, has not turned: a second dip counts the whole stretch, so no configuration leaves the clock by a short return to zero. With type C counterparties, a second dip below zero is cut at the next review. This is the specification's point 21.

- **Without it,** a configuration whose clock started in its first rounds, before the flow back caught up, and that then stood net-positive to the end, would count at the run's length, over the short span of its first rounds. A configuration that had stopped taking would read as one that never stopped.
- **Theorem VI.2 says such a configuration may persist:** it is in the net-positive class. The registry's note already says the same of an operator alone: one "that is cut off and then covers its own drain alone is no longer in a net-negative configuration, and its survival does not fire the switch."

**A defector that its hosts' records do not see taking.** In the dry run's random cell at m = 5, seed 2,000,056, a defector kept four of its six hosts from round 5 to the end, and stood at 213.9. From round 5, its hosts' records on their links to it, added together, were at zero or above in every round: the passive flow from its stock, higher than theirs, paid them back for what they gave. So by one round's record its configuration never had a clock, and it was in no figure. Under this text, its clock starts only if its counterparties' records over the window go below zero, and once the flow back holds them at zero or above to its end, it has turned net-positive. B2 judges by the record, as the registry's clock does, not by an operator's type. Such a defector's standing is what B2's claim says persists, and it fires nothing. It is reported by name, in every cell where it arises, with its stock and its hosts' records, so that a reader sees a type D operator still standing.

**A counterparty that cannot cut.** Since no link joins two defectors, a defector's counterparties are all of type C. But a type C operator can have a defector among its counterparties. With passive flow on, the defector's record on that link falls below zero where the defector's stock is above the type C operator's by more than about 90, at the default give: the flow from it then outweighs what it receives. A defector never cuts. So where any counterparty whose record over the window is below zero is a defector, part of what makes the configuration net-negative falls on an operator that cannot read, condition or cut. That is outside Theorem VI.2's premises. Such a configuration is reported in full, left out of the measure, and fires nothing.

**The mismatch sink, counted as the clock counts it.** Theorem VI.1's tax between two honest operators, each still paying its own drift, is not an extraction. Theorem VI.2 counts the sink as net-negative only when it is "driven past the taxed agent's maintenance margin". The clock's line is not the theorem's. The clock counts a sink as soon as the taxed operator's record on the link, over the window, is below zero, and that comes sooner: a rich operator's record can go below zero long before the tax threatens it. So the clock watches more than the theorem counts. A taxed operator of type C cuts on the same line, at its next review. That is the stand-in for Debt D1 at work: a record measures what passes through a link, not whether the operator can bear it. A steady sink whose counterparties' records over the window stay at zero or above is not watched, and fires nothing. A sink that is net-negative by the record, and still stands past its bound, counts against B2. For every watched sink, the run also reports its counterparties' records in the round its clock started with the passive flow left out, so that the tax is seen apart from what the gives did.

**The measure, for each seed:** the largest survival in spans among the configurations it watches. KS-COUP.1's note says a configuration persists if it "stays off the no-return surface, or keeps drawing net inflow through a link from a counterparty, beyond that bound". Any one that does is enough, so a seed's worst configuration is its measure. Beside it, every packet gives B1's measure, the largest among the configurations a defector is in, for comparison only.

**A configuration's end.** Where a watched configuration ends, by a cut, a new link or a ruin, and its counterparties' records on their links to the operator, added together over the last w rounds, are below zero just before it ends, its span is the counterparties' span, with their net loss taken at every sample, where that span is the shorter. Where it is not, the span of points 2, 3 and 14 stands. This is point 19, applied at every end, and not only at the run's end: the specification's point 22. It, and point 19 with it, can only shorten a span. They never lengthen one. As first worded, they did. In the world page's own case, the pair from the moment B defects, point 22 gave the longer span, and so changed a test value. Their purpose is a span that settling has made too long. Survival is counted in spans, so a shorter span makes it larger. So these two points can only make a fire, or a control's lasting, easier to show. They can never hide one. A configuration that lasts settles, and a settled whole's loss touches zero. So without it, one that lasted long and then ended would keep point 14's longest span, and could still read near zero. AG Fable found that case in reading B1. In B2's cells that can fire it cannot change a verdict, since nothing watched with counterparties lasts past the window. In the control it can, and there it lets a configuration that lasted show as lasting.

**A configuration still standing at the end:**

- **If its counterparties' records over the window are below zero,** its span is theirs, as above: point 19.
- **If it has turned net-positive,** it is judged to its turn, as above: point 21.
- **If its bound has already passed,** it counts at the run's length. That leans towards a fire, never away from one.
- **If its bound has not yet come,** it is "too long to judge in this run". A seed with any such configuration is left out of the mean whole, counted and reported.
- **If more than one seed in ten is left out,** the cell settles nothing, and says why.

**In a control,** every seed left out is counted as zero, and the control is judged on all its seeds. A seed left out as too long to judge holds a configuration that is still standing, so it can only hold more lasting, never less. A seed left with nothing to watch holds none. Counted so, a control can only understate what it shows. A control reads "no configuration lasts" only as before. Each control's result gives both means: over the seeds measured, and with every seed left out counted as zero.

**Near balance,** the theorem's own bound runs past any run. Theorem VI.2 calls the balance point "the non-sustained boundary of the net-positive class, settled by almost-sure recurrence of the zero-mean budget walk, not finite expected time", and the registry's note puts it outside. Under this text's readings, nothing watched with type C counterparties can stay near balance, since it ends within the window. In the control, what stays near balance to the end, and is still net-negative there, reads "too long to judge", and says so.

**A configuration ended by its type C counterparties' cut,** within the record window, scores no survival less the window. It can never be a seed's largest.


## 7. The judgement, cell by cell

**First, the premise check,** seed by seed. Every operator but the defectors is of type C, with the three premises. Drift is irreversible: every drift is above zero. No link joins two defectors. A watched configuration that a defector's own record helps make net-negative is outside the premises, as section 6 says: it is reported, and left out of the measure. A seed left with nothing to watch is left out of the mean, counted and reported, and it counts towards the one in ten. Every seed has defectors, linked or alone, so every seed is expected to have something to watch. A cell where the premises fail fires nothing.

**Then, on the mean of the measure across the seeds:**

| What the seeds show | What it means |
|---|---|
| The mean survival, plus two standard errors, at or under three spans | B2 holds in this cell, and the switch holds |
| The mean survival, less two standard errors, over three spans | The switch fires in this cell, subject to section 10 |
| Between the two | Settles nothing. Seeds 200,101 to 200,200 are added, once. If it is still between, it is reported as settling nothing. |

**Each cell is judged on its own, and every cell is reported.** No cell is added after a result is seen.

**The second sentence of the claim, shown, not judged.** What remains at the end of a long run is net-positive. Each run reports, at its end, the share of live links whose records over their last w rounds are above zero for both ends; every live link that is not, with its records; and the stock of every operator still standing. A type C operator reviews its links at every round's start, and cuts any whose record over its last w rounds is below zero for it. So at the last review, every live link was at zero or above over its last w rounds for every type C end. Only the last round, which no review has read, can move that. That is the premises at work, and is labelled so. And net-positive here is the record's. Whether a link delivers "structured energy exceeding the drift drain it must cover", in the theorem's words, is Debt D1's to state, and the record stands in for it.

**Falls that fire nothing.** An operator that falls while every link it has is at zero or above by the record is in no net-negative configuration by the record. Every such fall is reported, with the operator's links and drift, and fires nothing.


## 8. The largest of many, measured before freezing

## 9. Two engines

The second engine runs all 64 cells that can fire, with its own seeds.

- **They agree** when the difference of their means is within three of their combined standard errors. By chance alone, that is exceeded in about one cell in 370. Across 64 cells, one such by chance is not unlikely.
- **A cell where they disagree** is run again in both engines, once, with a second hundred seeds each: 300,101 to 300,200 for the second engine, and 200,101 to 200,200 for the reference. If the two hundred agree, it is recorded as chance. If not, it is reported as "engines disagree", the cause is looked for in the open, and no cell of B2 is judged until it is found and recorded.
- **Across the 64 cells,** how many the second engine is above, and how many below, is reported beside the rule.

**Before B2 is frozen, the two engines are read where they part.**

- **In B1's 30 cells judged by the rule,** every one agreed, but the second engine's mean was the lower in 25, by up to 4 parts in 100. In random links it was the lower in all 16. The cells share their seeds, so no chance figure is given for it. Each engine draws a seed's network first, so the same hundred networks run in every random cell of that engine, and one draw of networks can move all 16 together. The dry cell at m = 5, on a thousand seeds in each engine, found the two 0.33 combined standard errors apart.
- **In B2's dry run,** on B2's measure, the second engine was the higher: 0.3633 against 0.3357, 2.69 combined standard errors apart. That is within the rule of three. The plainest difference was in the type C operators left alone: 139 in the second engine against 91 in the reference, over a hundred seeds each. The gap was there before uneven drift: in the same cell with even drift, over a hundred seeds, B2's measure was 1.78 combined standard errors apart, and the operators left alone 3.1.
- **Neither is a result,** and each may be chance, since the two engines draw different random numbers.
- **So each is looked at where it sits,** on dry cells. In each engine, every operator left alone is counted by how it came to be alone: with no link as drawn, by its own cuts, by a counterparty's cut, or by a counterparty's ruin. Beside that, the number the drawn networks alone would give, and every defector's configuration by how it ended. And one of B1's random cells at m = 3 is run on a thousand dry seeds in each engine, as a check, never a result, to see whether the lean holds over more networks.
- **Where a difference of reading is found,** it is closed in the specification's text, and both engines follow it. B1's cells are then run again on both, as a check, never a result, to show their verdicts unchanged.
- **Where none is found,** the gap in operators left alone is bounded. Both engines run D1 again, on a thousand dry seeds each, and the gap's size, with its standard error, and what it moves in B2's measure, are given here before the freeze. A gap that wide is not left as chance.

**What was found.** No difference of reading in the measure. The gap is the networks each engine drew. One reported count, how many configurations one round's record alone would have watched, is read in the second engine from a configuration's opening inside a round, as that engine's own clock was before point 20. It is a count, never the measure.

- **In D1 and every random cell of an engine,** the same hundred networks run. The reference's hundred have 0.25 operators with no link and 0.36 hosts tied only to defectors, a seed. The law of the draw gives 0.39 and 0.44. The second engine's hundred come near the law, at 0.42 and 0.49. One draw of networks moves every random cell of an engine together.

## 10. What a result does

- **B2 holds in a cell:** it is recorded, and the switch stands.
- **A cell fires:** Phase 0's rule, Part Four. It is shown at once as "reported, being checked". It is rerun from its packet, and checked against the premises. AG Fable reads it, and G decides it for the registry. A switch that fires stays fired.
- **A cell settles nothing:** it is reported, with why.

**Which switch a fire reaches.** KS-COUP.1 is B2's switch. A cell that fires names, seed by seed, the configuration that set its measure, and its kind. Where those are defectors', with their hosts, or a defector left alone after them, KS-BAR.9 fires with KS-COUP.1, since the registry gives the two one bound. A sink's configuration, an operator alone that is not a defector, or a defector that never had a link, reaches KS-COUP.1 alone. That last is outside KS-BAR.9, whose note says "The system must be coupled".

**Reported for every cell, whatever the judgement:**

- every watched configuration's survival in spans, as a number, its kind, and which span it used;
- where each of points 14, 19, 21 and 22 applied;
- the number of configurations each defector passed through, and how many defectors were ruined;
- every configuration that lasted more than one span, measured as section 6 measures survival, and then ended;
- every configuration that returned to zero or above and later went below zero again;
- every cut of one type C operator by another;
- every configuration left out as outside the premises, in full;
- every defector still standing at the end, with its stock and its hosts' records;
- every fall that fires nothing;
- how many configurations one round's record alone, the counterparties' records added together, would have watched;
- how many seeds were left out, and why, and the premise check;
- at the end, the share of live links net-positive for both ends, and the stock of every operator still standing;
- and both engines' results, and the control's in full.

A control cell is never printed as "fires" or "holds". It is printed as "a configuration lasts", with its kind, or "no configuration lasts", with "control: fires nothing" beside it.

**Every packet goes into the record,** labelled as the desks' own run. Group B is never mixed with Group A.
