# The registry · a dated addition after B1, with B2's cases · for version 5.41

**From AG Opus, for G, then AG Fable and WC · 14 November 2026 · version 6, with B2's cases, AG Fable's three sentences on an operator that never gives, and KS-32.7's line for AP32's dated note**

The registry's notes bound the coupling road's two switches, KS-COUP.1 and KS-BAR.9, by three spans of the configuration. A run of the world reaches cases that the notes do not state. Version 3 of this addition carried two of them, which B1 met: the mixed case, and a configuration still standing at the end. It said that if B2 added a case to how a run is measured, it would go into this same note, so the registry carries one note, not two. B2 adds three:

- **Which record the clock reads.** The notes say the clock starts when the operator "takes through them more than it gives", and not over which rounds. With passive flow on, one round's record and the record window part. B2 reads the window: the record a defended counterparty acts on.
- **A configuration whose links turn net-positive,** without a cut. Without this case, a configuration that had stopped taking would read as one that never stopped, and could fire the switch. It holds whatever the operator's kind. So an operator that never gives, whose counterparties' records stand at zero or above, fires nothing. That narrows what the two switches fire on, so it is said in the open, with KS-COUP.1's words in AP02 beside it, "pure defection persists against a defended counterparty". G kept this reading on 14 November 2026, before any cell of B2 ran.
- **A configuration's end, whenever it comes.** Version 3 took the counterparties' span for a configuration still standing at the end of a run. A configuration that lasted long and then ended needs the same, as AG Fable found in reading B1.

And one line of scope, since B2 watches more than defectors: KS-BAR.9's note says one run "can never fire one switch and not the other". That is true of a defector's configuration. A run that also watches configurations that are no defector's can fire KS-COUP.1 alone.

A switch must be fired, or held, by its own words in the registry, not by a rule the registry does not carry. So these go into the notes before B2 is frozen. The world's specification, version 1.1, carries the same cases, in its Part Nine, points 14, 19, 20, 21 and 22.

And the registry records B1's run, with B1's own label: the premises at work, not a test the switches could have failed.

And one line goes on KS-32.7's row, as AP32's dated note set out: the note was filed on 14 November 2026, and the registry moves once, with this addition.

> KS-COUP.1, dated addition, 14 November 2026 (MKSR v5.41). Four cases, stated, for a run of a finite world. The clock: the operator's links turn net-negative for its counterparties when their records on their links to it, each link read over its last record window, added together, are below zero: the record a defended counterparty acts on. Where no passive flow runs, a defector's hosts' records are below zero in every round, and every reading of the record agrees. The mixed case: where the configuration's whole loses at some samples and gains at others, the span at each sample is the whole's where it loses, and the counterparties' where it gains; the configuration's span is the longest of the spans that applied, so the bound never fires because a span was measured short. A configuration whose links turn net-positive: where its counterparties' records on their links to the operator, read as the clock reads them, return to zero or above, and stay there to its end, it has turned net-positive by the record, without a cut; from then it is no longer a net-negative configuration, and what it does after does not fire the switch, as with an operator that is cut off and then covers its own drain alone. This holds whatever the operator's kind, and even where the operator still draws net inflow through its links: with a delivery worth more than it costs, both ends of a link can gain. In Theorem VI.2's list, pure defection is a kind of net-negative configuration, and so it is read in this switch's words, "pure defection persists against a defended counterparty": a sustained defector is one whose links stay net-negative for its counterparties. An operator that never gives, whose counterparties' records stand at zero or above, is reported by name in every run. The end: where a configuration ends, or is still standing at the end of a finite run, and its counterparties' records on their links to the operator, added together over the last record window, are below zero, its span is the counterparties' span, with their net loss taken at every sample; a configuration that lasts settles, and without this its span would run past any run, where persisting could never show. An operator alone has no counterparty, and neither of the last two applies to it. Every run reports where each case applied. On a defector's configuration, with its counterparties or left alone after them, this switch and KS-BAR.9 share one bound, and fire or hold together; a configuration that is no defector's bears on this switch alone.
