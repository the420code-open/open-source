# How to take part

**Three ways in.** A minute: try the floor, and tell us what happened. An hour: run one test, or reproduce one result. A week: build something on the work, or run the world in your own engine.

**How to send.** Choose Send your work, in the Open Source room on the family's sites. Each form asks only what the work needs. There is nothing to sign up for. Prefer email? Write to open@studiog.global, and we will put it into the record for you, without your address. If you already work on GitHub, the forms under Issues do the same.

**What happens next.** You get a thank-you at once. You get a first reply within 7 days. Everything you send is shown in the open record, labelled as from outside, with its status and its date. A serious challenge is shown at once. It never waits for the work's answer.

**What a result needs.** Name the kill switch it tests, by its code, and the registry version you read it from. Send the whole run: every seed, every run, not only the good ones. Upload a small run packet with the form, or link to where it is kept, with the fingerprint of each file. A result that fires a switch is the system doing what it was built to do. It is shown, checked by running it again, and never repaired.

**The terms.** What you send is shared under the same free terms as everything built here: CC0 for text and data, Zero-Clause BSD or MIT for code, at the user's choice. The 420 Code itself is never altered. You may quote it. You may build on it.

**Three labels, and only three.** Built on the Embryo: for a build that keeps the floor and passes its hard-floor tests. A test by another hand: for any test or run. Approved: for what I have read and approved. Nothing else may present itself as the work's own.

**Credit.** You are credited by the name or handle you give. Ask for none, and you get none.

**Who decides what.** I am G, the writer of the work, and Studio G is my studio. Studio G keeps this repository. It uses AI, openly, to read, sort and check what comes in, and to run results again. The status of what you send is decided here, and every decision is dated. I approve what appears as the work's own words, and any name on one of the work's pages. Nobody else does.

**Conduct.** Criticism of the work, however hard, is welcome. The rules are in CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md. They govern conduct, never agreement.

**If we decline.** You are told why. You may correct an honest mistake and send again. You may contest the decision, with a comment on your contribution's own page.
